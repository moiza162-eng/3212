Social media

Build a social content calendar from customer questions

Collect the questions you already hear

Review enquiries, comments and common questions from colleagues. Group them into recurring decisions such as cost, eligibility and what happens after signup. Use real patterns without exposing private messages or personal information. Choose three topics that help people take the next step.

Turn each question into a useful post

A question about classroom attendance could become a short video showing the venue and explaining which sessions are online. A question about eligibility might work better as a carousel with an example and a link to the full criteria. Put the answer up front, add captions to video and give the post a clear destination when people need more detail.

Review after a manageable trial

Try a two-week calendar and track the topic, format, intended action and result. Compare saves for practical guides, qualified clicks for registration posts and recurring comments for missing information. Use those signals to plan the next set of posts. Keep an idea bank so seasonal announcements do not crowd out the questions people ask year-round.

Build an idea bank from observed demand

Set aside a short session to review recurring enquiries with front-line staff. Ask which missing answers lead to phone calls and which common misunderstandings delay decisions. Write each question in the language people use and attach the current source of truth, such as an eligibility page or course guide. Put temporary questions about dates in a separate group so the team knows when to retire them. Prioritise subjects that matter to the audience and that you can answer accurately. The idea bank gives you a starting point when a campaign ends or a planned post can no longer be published.

Choose a format that suits the answer

Use a short clip when seeing a process or a place helps someone understand it. A course venue walkthrough might start at the entrance and show what an arrival looks like. A carousel can work for a sequence of steps, such as gathering registration information and checking entry criteria. A short text post may be enough to explain a change to opening hours. Include captions where needed and make links or next steps easy to find. Check the answer against the verified course details before publishing. If you use a scenario to explain eligibility, label it as an example and point to the full rules.

Give each post a role in the week

Build a calendar with the question, format, owner, due date and review date. Balance timely announcements with reusable answers so the account stays helpful between launches. If the team can produce two thoughtful posts a week, plan for that capacity and leave space for urgent changes. Note how someone will reach the related page from the platform. A link in a profile or a pinned post only helps if the destination is obvious and kept current. Ask a colleague to check the complete path on a phone. They should be able to find the answer and the next step without relying on background knowledge.

Use a short review to improve the next plan

Look at a small set of signals tied to each post's purpose. For a quick guide, saves or follow-up questions may tell you more than reach. For a registration post, look at relevant visits and completed actions if they can be measured. Read comments and enquiries for evidence that people understood the message. If a popular post brings low-quality clicks, examine whether its hook promised something the destination could not deliver. Turn the review into one practical experiment for the next cycle. For instance, put the venue in the opening line of a video about in-person classes, then see whether fewer viewers ask where the course is held.

Keep a copy of the answers that have already been approved so the team can reuse them in comments, replies and future posts. When an answer changes, update the shared version and check whether any pinned post still uses the old wording. That routine helps people see consistent guidance wherever they encounter the account that week. It also gives new team members a useful reference when they need to reply promptly to questions about the same topic.
