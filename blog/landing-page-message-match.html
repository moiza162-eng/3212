Conversion

Match your landing page to the promise in your ad

Write down the promise

Look at the headline, audience and offer in the ad, then open the destination on a phone. A message about a free Level 2 course for eligible Londoners should land on a page that quickly explains the level, who qualifies and what to do next. A general training catalogue makes visitors work to confirm they are in the right place.

Remove the unanswered questions

Read the page as someone seeing the organisation for the first time. Can you find the price or funding criteria, where classes take place and the time commitment? Can you tell whether the form is an application or an expression of interest? Put the answers near the main action and make the button label describe what happens next.

Test the whole journey

Submit the form on a phone and a desktop, including one attempt with missing information. Confirm that errors are legible, successful submission is acknowledged and the enquiry reaches its intended destination. Compare completion rates and lead quality after the update. If traffic is limited, combine analytics with short usability sessions and record where people hesitate.

Build a simple message map

Take the ads sending most of the traffic and note each audience, offer and destination. Include the exact action the ad suggests. A Learn more ad sets up a different expectation from a Book now ad even when they promote the same course. Compare those promises with the first screen on a small phone. Check whether the visitor can recognise the course or service, establish basic suitability and see the next step without hunting for it. Repeat for any ad targeted to a different location or audience. If the promises differ materially, decide whether one page can serve both clearly.

Put the practical details where people need them

A visitor often has one practical objection before they feel ready to act. If they are unsure whether they qualify, show the main eligibility criteria before asking for contact details. If they need to arrange childcare or travel, give them a schedule and attendance format. Use a short summary near the main action and a fuller explanation lower down for people who need it. Avoid inventing urgency when a start date or deadline has not been confirmed. A registration page can say that the organisation will confirm availability after receiving the form, so the reader understands what they are agreeing to.

Review the form as part of the page

Ask only for information needed to take the next step. Use labels that stay visible while typing and explain unfamiliar requirements. On mobile, check that users can reach the page action and recover from an error without losing previous input. The success message should tell them whether they have registered, made an enquiry or requested a callback, and when they can expect to hear back if a timeframe is known. Add a contact route for people who cannot use the form. Before changing fields, ask the people who receive submissions which details they actually use. Changes that reduce friction are most valuable when the receiving team can still act on the enquiry.

Define what the test should improve

Pick one useful outcome, such as suitable course enquiries per landing page visit. Record the current traffic sources and use the same definition after release. If enquiry volume rises but fewer people meet the entry requirements, the page may need a clearer explanation of who the offer is for. If people start the form but leave at the same point, observe that step on a phone and look for a confusing request or error. Test the full route with someone unfamiliar with the ad before committing more budget. Give them the ad and a realistic task, then ask what they expected after each step. The mismatch often appears in their own words.

Keep the old wording and the reason for the change in your test notes. If a future campaign changes the offer again, the team can see which details were deliberately moved closer to the form. That history is especially helpful when several teams share one landing page and each has a different idea of what the reader needs to see before registering online.
