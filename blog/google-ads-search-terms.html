Paid search

A weekly Google Ads search-term review

Read the queries behind the spend

Start with search terms that received enough traffic to matter. Group them by apparent purpose and compare cost, conversions and conversion quality where available. A training provider selling paid exam bookings may see searches from people looking for free revision materials. Those two audiences often need different landing pages and different budgets.

Exclude carefully

For clearly irrelevant queries, choose a negative keyword at the level that matches the problem. A term irrelevant to one ad group might be valuable elsewhere. Check the proposed negative against your active keyword and landing page themes before applying it. Document the change and why you made it, especially if multiple people manage the account.

Use useful surprises

If a recurring relevant term drives good enquiries, check whether your ad and landing page speak directly to it. A more specific ad message or a clear price and booking explanation may be the best next test. Review the changes after enough data has accumulated and give recent conversions time to be recorded before comparing performance.

Start with a consistent review window

Use a date range that provides enough activity and leave time for delayed conversions to appear. Note changes in budget, bidding, location settings or the landing page before comparing one period to another. Export the available terms with their campaign and ad group, then sort by cost and look for repeated patterns. Group similar queries around what people seem to want. Someone seeking revision materials, someone comparing training providers and someone ready to book an exam can use similar words while expecting very different outcomes. Record the most important theme in plain language so colleagues can understand why you chose to act.

Use lead quality alongside conversion counts

A query that appears expensive may still be valuable if it brings suitable enquiries. Ask the team that handles those enquiries what makes a lead qualified, then compare themes against that definition where your reporting allows it. If the account counts button clicks or page visits as conversions, note that limitation clearly. Those signals can help investigate a journey, but they do not establish whether people booked. When there are too few conversions to judge a term fairly, look at its relevance and check back after more activity. Avoid closing down an entire query theme simply because one week produced no confirmed sales.

Match the response to the problem

If a theme is unrelated across the entire campaign, a campaign-level negative might make sense. If it belongs in another ad group, protect the relevant group when adding an exclusion. Review how the negative match type behaves and recheck existing search themes before saving the change. For a legitimate theme with weak performance, inspect the ad promise and destination first. A page designed for course enquiries may leave exam candidates wondering where to book. In that case the action could be a better destination, clearer eligibility or a separate test rather than an exclusion. Record the rationale and intended effect.

Track a small set of changes

Maintain a log with the date, the affected campaign, the search theme and what you expect to improve. For an exclusion, check whether spend moves away from the irrelevant theme and whether relevant traffic remains healthy. For a page or ad change, review enquiry quality and conversion cost after an appropriate observation period. Search term reports may show only a subset of activity, so compare the campaign totals as well. Review the results with someone who understands the commercial objective. When you discover a new query worth pursuing, capture the intended audience and next action in the same log. That keeps the weekly review connected to decisions about budget and landing pages.

Set aside time for a second reviewer when a change you are considering could exclude a large class of searches. Show them a few representative queries you want to keep and a few you want to block. Ask them to explain why each belongs in its group. A disagreement is a useful prompt to revisit the page, offer or account structure before editing the campaign. Save the examples with the change record so future reviewers know what the exclusion was originally meant to accomplish.
