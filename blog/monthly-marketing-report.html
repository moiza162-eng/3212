Reporting

Write a monthly marketing report that leads to decisions

Begin with the decision on the table

Work backwards from the decision a manager needs to make, such as whether to focus next month's effort on course discovery or registration. Summarise the relevant trend in a few sentences. Define the metric and comparison period so another person can reproduce your conclusion.

Put unusual changes in context

Show a short time series, then explain what may have affected it: a new cohort launch, different paid spend or a tracking change. If registrations increased when both search traffic and advertising activity rose, describe what you can attribute and what needs further analysis. Small counts are often clearer as raw numbers alongside percentages.

Close with owners and checks

Choose two or three concrete actions and name the person and the date responsible for each. For example, ask the web team to improve the eligibility section before the next campaign and have analytics confirm the registration event after release. At the next review, check whether the actions shipped, what evidence appeared and whether the original decision should change.
